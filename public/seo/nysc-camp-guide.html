NYSC camp guide: what to pack, what to expect

The packing list, the daily routine, and the small things nobody tells you before your three weeks in khaki.

Packing listArriving at campDaily routineSurvival tipsAfter campFAQ
Requirements change from batch to batch. Your call-up letter and the official NYSC portal always come first; this guide fills in the rest.

What to take to NYSC camp: the packing list

Documents

  • Printed call-up letter
  • Your statement of result or certificate, plus photocopies
  • School ID card and a government ID (NIN slip or similar)
  • Medical fitness certificate, if your call-up letter asks for it
  • Passport photographs on a white background (bring plenty)
  • A waterproof folder or envelope to keep all of it dry

Clothes

  • White T-shirts and white shorts (several of each)
  • White socks and white canvas/tennis shoes
  • Slippers for the hostel and bathroom
  • Comfortable casual clothes for evenings
  • Nightwear and enough underwear for a few days between washes
  • A sweater or jacket if you are posted somewhere cool, like Plateau

Hostel life

  • Bucket and bowl
  • Padlock and key for your box or locker
  • Bedsheet, pillow and a light blanket
  • Mosquito net or repellent
  • Rechargeable lamp or torch
  • Power bank (charging points get crowded)
  • Plate, spoon, cup and a small flask
  • Hangers, a rope or line, and washing powder

Personal care

  • Toiletries, towel and sanitary products
  • Any medication you take, with your prescription
  • Basic first aid: plasters, pain relief, ORS
  • Sunscreen or a cap for long hours on the parade ground

Money

  • Cash in small notes for the mammy market
  • Your bank app working and your BVN details handy
  • A little extra for transport after camp

Arriving at camp

At the gate, soldiers check your call-up letter and search your bag. Then comes registration: document checks, a thumbprint, your photographs and a room in the hostel. Kitting follows, where you collect your khaki, crested vest, white T-shirts and shorts, jungle boots and canvas. Expect queues at every step. Arrive early and keep your documents in one folder you can open quickly.

A day in camp

  1. Before sunrise. The bugle wakes the camp. Morning devotion, then out to the parade ground.
  2. Morning. Parade drills, Man O’ War training, then breakfast.
  3. Midday. Lectures in the hall: NYSC rules, security, health and career talks.
  4. Afternoon. SAED sessions and platoon activities.
  5. Evening. Sports, social nights, and the mammy market for food, charging and gist.
  6. Night. Back to the hostel for lights out. Tomorrow starts early again.

Survival tips

  • Label everything you own, especially white clothes and buckets.
  • Join your platoon group chat, but confirm important news with officials before acting on it.
  • Drink water and use sunscreen. Parade grounds get hot.
  • Keep a power bank charged; the charging queue at the mammy market is long at night.
  • Make friends across platoons. They will be your information network after camp.
  • Rest when you can. Camp is short, and the days start very early.

After camp: PPA, CDS and clearance

At the end of camp you get a posting letter. Report to your PPA, submit your acceptance letter, and get to know your LGA’s NYSC office and LGI. Every week there is CDS with your group. Every month there is clearance, and your allowance depends on it. Then, at the end of the year, POP. Words you don’t know yet? Check the NYSC slang dictionary, or find your state on the states page.

Questions corpers ask

How long is NYSC orientation camp?

Orientation camp is about three weeks (21 days) at the start of the service year. Check your call-up letter for your exact dates.

What should I not bring to NYSC camp?

Many camps do not allow heating appliances like electric kettles, pressing irons or cooking stoves in the hostels. Rules vary, so check your camp’s instructions.

What happens after camp?

You get a posting letter to a PPA in one of the state’s LGAs. From then on you work at your PPA, attend CDS weekly, and do clearance at your LGA office every month until POP.

Do I need to buy white clothes before camp?

Your kit includes some white items, but most corpers bring extra white T-shirts, shorts, socks and canvas shoes because you wear them every day and laundry takes time.
