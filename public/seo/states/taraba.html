Serving in Taraba State: camp, LGAs and corper life

Your call-up letter says Taraba. Here is what that means: where the orientation camp is, which LGAs you can be posted to, and what daily life looks like in Jalingo. Then play it, on real Jalingo streets.

The Taraba orientation camp

Corpers deployed to Taraba State report to the orientation camp at Sibre, Airport Road, Jalingo for about three weeks of registration, kitting, swearing-in, parade, SAED lectures and evenings at the mammy market. At the end of camp, everyone gets a posting letter to a PPA in one of the state's LGAs.

Camp locations can change between batches, for example when a camp is being renovated or for security reasons. Always confirm with your call-up letter and the official NYSC portal before you travel.

In Kopa Life your service year starts at the camp gate too. A soldier checks your call-up letter, you queue for registration, collect your khaki and jungle boots, and learn drills with your platoon. Our NYSC camp guide has the full packing list and daily routine.

LGAs in Taraba you can be posted to

Taraba State has 16 local government areas. Your posting letter names one of them, and that LGA's NYSC office is where you do monthly clearance and meet your LGI. Busier, more urban areas are outlined in green.

  • Ardo-Kola
  • Bali
  • Donga
  • Gashaka
  • Gassol
  • Ibi
  • Jalingo
  • Karim-Lamido
  • Kurmi
  • Lau
  • Sardauna
  • Takum
  • Ussa
  • Wukari
  • Yorro
  • Zing

1 of 16 is a busier urban area in the game: more corpers, longer clearance queues and a louder group chat.

Corper life in Jalingo

The weather is hot days and dusty harmattan mornings. To get around, corpers use keke (tricycles), okada (motorbikes) and buses. In the game, food and transport prices in Taraba are among the cheapest in the game.

Local languages you will hear: Hausa / Kanuri / Fulfulde. Expect greetings like “Sannu da zuwa, corper!” and “Ina kwana?”. At the buka you will find Tuwo masara & miyan kuka, Masa & miyan taushe, Kilishi and Fura da nono, with Fura da nono to wash it down.
