Serving in Rivers State: camp, LGAs and corper life

Your call-up letter says Rivers. Here is what that means: where the orientation camp is, which LGAs you can be posted to, and what daily life looks like in Port Harcourt. Then play it, on real Port Harcourt streets.

The Rivers orientation camp

Corpers deployed to Rivers State report to the orientation camp at Nonwa-Gbam, Tai LGA for about three weeks of registration, kitting, swearing-in, parade, SAED lectures and evenings at the mammy market. At the end of camp, everyone gets a posting letter to a PPA in one of the state's LGAs.

Camp locations can change between batches, for example when a camp is being renovated or for security reasons. Always confirm with your call-up letter and the official NYSC portal before you travel.

In Kopa Life your service year starts at the camp gate too. A soldier checks your call-up letter, you queue for registration, collect your khaki and jungle boots, and learn drills with your platoon. Our NYSC camp guide has the full packing list and daily routine.

Corper life in Port Harcourt

The weather is hot and humid most of the year. To get around, corpers use drop taxis, keke (tricycles) and buses. In the game, food and transport prices in Rivers are on the high side, close to Lagos.

Port Harcourt "drop" taxis, soot on the windscreen, and fish pepper soup.

Local languages you will hear: Pidgin. Expect greetings like “How far, corper?” and “Abeg welcome!”. At the buka you will find Banga soup & starch, Bole & fish, Afang soup & eba and Edikaikong, with Chapman to wash it down.
