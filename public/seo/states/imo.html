Serving in Imo State: camp, LGAs and corper life

Your call-up letter says Imo. Here is what that means: where the orientation camp is, which LGAs you can be posted to, and what daily life looks like in Owerri. Then play it, on real Owerri streets.

The Imo orientation camp

Corpers deployed to Imo State report to the orientation camp at Eziama Obaire, Nkwerre LGA for about three weeks of registration, kitting, swearing-in, parade, SAED lectures and evenings at the mammy market. At the end of camp, everyone gets a posting letter to a PPA in one of the state's LGAs.

Camp locations can change between batches, for example when a camp is being renovated or for security reasons. Always confirm with your call-up letter and the official NYSC portal before you travel.

In Kopa Life your service year starts at the camp gate too. A soldier checks your call-up letter, you queue for registration, collect your khaki and jungle boots, and learn drills with your platoon. Our NYSC camp guide has the full packing list and daily routine.

Corper life in Owerri

The weather is hot and humid most of the year. To get around, corpers use keke (tricycles), okada (motorbikes) and buses. In the game, food and transport prices in Imo are cheaper than Lagos.

Local languages you will hear: Igbo. Expect greetings like “Corper, nnọọ!” and “Kedụ?”. At the buka you will find Abacha & ugba, Okpa, Ofe onugbu & fufu and Nkwobi, with Palm wine (no be for PPA o) to wash it down.
